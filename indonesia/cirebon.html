Cirebon: 10 places for sultan's palaces, batik and street food

Old sultanate courts, a cave-like water garden, a red-brick mosque from the Arab quarter, batik villages and a clay-pot beef soup that locals swear by - Cirebon is Java's north-coast sultanate city, and a rewarding stop between Jakarta and Central Java. The 10 places not to miss

Chosen for first-time and returning visitors. Add the ones you like to your trip - we turn them into a plan.

#01Sultan's palaceLemahwungkuk

Kasepuhan Palace (Keraton Kasepuhan)

The senior of Cirebon's royal courts, with roots in the 1500s, where the sultan's family still keeps old traditions alive. Red-brick walls and gates, an open pavilion and a small museum hold royal heirlooms, including a ceremonial carriage shaped like a mythical creature.

  • ⏱ 1.5 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipGo in the morning when it is cooler. Opening times can change for royal ceremonies and holidays, and local guides are available at the entrance - we check for your date.
📷 Mokhammad Misdianto · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#02Royal courtsPekalipan

Kanoman Palace & Kacirebonan

The second court, which split from Kasepuhan in the late 1600s, sits beside a busy traditional market. Its museum shows royal carriages, among them a winged, trunked and dragon-like beast. The smaller Kacirebonan court, from the early 1800s, is close by.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 about 5-10 min from Kasepuhan
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipThe market outside is lively and a good place for snacks and photos. Which parts of each palace are open depends on the day and on court events, so we confirm for your dates.
📷 LintasGiat · CC0 · Wikimedia Commons
#03Water gardenKesambi

Taman Sunyaragi Water Palace & Caves

A royal garden of rock-like towers, tunnels, chambers and small pools that looks like a coral maze, traditionally linked to the 1700s. The sultans used it as a quiet retreat for rest and meditation, and today it is Cirebon's most photogenic stop.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 about 15 min from the centre
  • 🌅 Morning or late afternoon
Our tipThe passages are narrow with uneven steps, so wear good shoes. Midday sun is harsh on the open stone; early or late is more comfortable.
📷 Pijri Paijar · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#04Old mosqueBeside Kasepuhan

Sang Cipta Rasa Great Mosque

One of Java's older mosques, traditionally dated to the late 1400s and still in daily use. Its simple tiered roof, thick walls and carved details reflect the early Islamic-Javanese style of the north coast.

  • ⏱ 45 min
  • 🚗 next to Kasepuhan Palace
  • 🌅 Morning, outside prayer times
Our tipVisitors are welcome outside prayer times; dress modestly and be quiet inside. Entry rules for non-worshippers can vary, so ask politely at the gate.
📷 Bellmaker5698 · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#05Old quarterPanjunan

Panjunan Arab Quarter & Red Mosque

A neighbourhood where Arab traders once settled beside the old harbour. Its small red-brick mosque, traditionally dated to the 1400s, shows how Chinese, Javanese and Islamic touches blended in Cirebon's early port.

  • ⏱ 1 hour
  • 🚗 about 10 min from the centre
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipIt is a living neighbourhood, so keep voices low and ask before photographing residents. Combine it with the Chinese temple and the palaces for an old-town morning.
📷 Pijri Paijar · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#06Chinese templeOld town

Dewi Welas Asih Temple

One of Cirebon's oldest Chinese temples, near the old port area, dedicated to the goddess of mercy. Red lanterns, incense and carved beams recall the Chinese community that has lived in the city for centuries.

  • ⏱ 45 min
  • 🚗 in the old town, near the centre
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipIt is a place of worship: dress modestly, keep quiet and ask before photographing prayers. Busiest around Chinese festival days.
📷 Pijri Paijar · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#07Pilgrimage siteAstana, north of the city

Sunan Gunung Jati Royal Tomb

The hilltop burial ground of Sunan Gunung Jati, one of the nine saints who spread Islam in Java and the founder of the Cirebon sultanate. Pilgrims come from all over Indonesia, and the walls are decorated with old ceramic plates.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 about 20-30 min north of the centre
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipThis is a sacred place: dress modestly, speak softly and follow the caretakers. Access to the inner areas is restricted and rules change, so we check what visitors may do on your dates.
📷 Cahyo Ramadhani · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#08BatikPlered, west of the city

Trusmi Batik Village

The home of Cirebon batik, famous for its 'mega mendung' cloud motif in bold colours. Small workshops line the road, and many let you watch the wax being drawn and dyed or try a little batik yourself.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 about 20-30 min from the centre
  • 🌅 Morning to early afternoon
Our tipAsk which pieces are hand-drawn or stamped and which are printed, since prices differ a lot. Workshops are busiest on weekends, and weekday mornings are calmer.
📷 Fpangestuphotographer · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#09FoodAround the old town

Cirebon food: empal gentong, nasi jamblang & tahu gejrot

Try empal gentong, a rich beef soup in coconut milk cooked in a clay pot, and nasi jamblang, rice served on a leaf with side dishes you pick yourself. Add tahu gejrot, fried tofu in a sweet-sour chilli-garlic dressing, and docang, a warm soup of rice cake and vegetables.

  • ⏱ 2 hours
  • 🚗 in and around the centre
  • 🌅 Lunch and evening
Our tipMost food is halal, but check what goes into each dish if you avoid certain ingredients. Lunchtime is the busy time for nasi jamblang, and spicy levels are easy to adjust - just ask.
📷 swara dila · CC BY 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#10History & highland airKuningan, foot of Mount Ciremai

Linggarjati Negotiation Building, Kuningan

A cool village below Mount Ciremai, where a house became the venue for talks between Indonesia and the Netherlands in 1946-47 - the Linggarjati agreement. It is now a small museum, with fresh mountain air and green views around it.

  • ⏱ Half day
  • 🚗 about 45-60 min south of Cirebon
  • 🌅 Morning, dry season
Our tipA half-day trip by car, easy to combine with a stop at a village on the way. Opening times can change, and the weather turns cloudy and rainy in the wet season.
📷 Sersan Mayor Kururu · CC BY 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons

Practical tips

Getting there

Express trains from Jakarta usually take roughly 3 hours to Cirebon, and the Trans-Java toll road takes about 3-4 hours by car depending on traffic. Kertajati airport (BJ Habibie, near Majalengka) serves the region, roughly an hour away, but flights change often, so we check what is running for your date.

Best time

The dry season, roughly April to October, is best for walking between palaces and for the Linggarjati hills. Cirebon is hot and humid all year on the coast, so visit sights in the morning or late afternoon and keep midday for lunch and rest.

Respect & dress

Cover shoulders and knees at palaces, mosques, temples and the royal tomb, speak softly and ask before photographing people at prayer. Cirebon is a deeply religious city, and Fridays and religious days may change what is open. Most local food is halal.

Getting around

The old-town sights are close together and easy by ride-hailing app, taxi or a becak (pedal trishaw) for short hops. Gunung Jati to the north, Trusmi to the west and Linggarjati to the south are spread out, so a car with driver is the easiest way to combine them.
