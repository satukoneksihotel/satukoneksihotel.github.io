The 10 places not to miss

Chosen for first-time and returning visitors. Add the ones you like to your trip - we turn them into a plan.

🏞️#01Royal palaceCity centre

Kraton Surakarta (Kasunanan Palace)

The royal court founded in the 1740s, still home to the Susuhunan's family. Blue-and-white pavilions, a museum of carriages and heirlooms, and gamelan music at times.

  • ⏱ 2 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipCover shoulders and knees. Only parts of the palace are open and ceremonies can close areas, so check the day's access. A guide explains the symbolism well.
#02Royal palaceKeprabon

Mangkunegaran Palace

Seat of the junior royal house, built in 1757. Visitors tour a grand open pavilion, a museum of jewellery, weapons and manuscripts, and gardens, usually with a guide.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipOpening times for visitors change, so we check them for your date. Pair it with the Triwindu market, which is a short walk away.
📷 Crisco 1492 · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#03BatikLaweyan & Kauman

Batik villages: Laweyan & Kauman

Two old batik quarters of narrow alleys and merchant houses. Watch workers draw wax patterns by hand, join a short workshop and shop directly from makers.

  • ⏱ 3 hours
  • 🚗 in the city, 10-20 min apart
  • 🌅 Morning, workshops open
Our tipWalk the alleys rather than drive. Ask what is hand-drawn (tulis), stamped (cap) or printed - prices differ a lot, and honest sellers will say which is which.
📷 WikiSanktGerard at English Wikipedia · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#04Market & foodSudiroprajan

Pasar Gede & Solo food

The city's main traditional market, near a Chinese temple, full of snacks and fresh produce. Around Solo try selat Solo (beef salad-steak), serabi pancakes and nasi liwet.

  • ⏱ 2 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre
  • 🌅 Morning, then evening for dinner
Our tipGo in the morning for the freshest snacks. Nasi liwet stalls in the Keprabon area are busiest in the evening. Ask us for halal-checked places.
📷 Puryono · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#05AntiquesNgarsopuro

Triwindu Antique Market

A treasure-hunt market of old lamps, vinyl, clocks, porcelain and Javanese curios, just beside Mangkunegaran along the Ngarsopuro street.

  • ⏱ 1 hour
  • 🚗 in the city centre
  • 🌅 Morning, or Saturday night nearby
Our tipBargain politely and ask if an antique may be exported. A night market with food stalls runs on Saturday evenings in front of it.
📷 Christophe95 · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#06MuseumSriwedari

Radya Pustaka Museum & Sriwedari

Opened in 1890 and the second-oldest museum in Indonesia, it holds Javanese court manuscripts, puppets, statues and old weapons, beside the historic Sriwedari park.

  • ⏱ 1 hour
  • 🚗 in the city centre
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipIt is a small, traditional museum, so a short visit is enough. Check which days it is open before you go.
📷 Meursault2004 · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#07WaterfallKaranganyar

Tawangmangu & Grojogan Sewu Waterfall

A cool mountain resort town on the slope of Mount Lawu, with a tall waterfall in a forest reached by a stepped path, plus tea gardens nearby.

  • ⏱ Half day
  • 🚗 about 1-1.5 hrs east of Solo
  • 🌅 Morning, dry season
Our tipExpect many steps back up. Keep food out of sight, as monkeys live around the path, and avoid the paths after heavy rain.
📷 Jmhullot · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#08Hill templeMount Lawu

Sukuh Temple

A 15th-century pyramid-shaped Hindu-Javanese temple on the slope of Mount Lawu, with rough carved reliefs and statues linked to fertility and ancestors.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 about 1-1.5 hrs east of Solo
  • 🌅 Morning, clear weather
Our tipSome carvings are explicit, so consider that for children. Roads are winding and often foggy; bring a light jacket and sturdy shoes.
📷 Anton Leddin · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#09Hill templeMount Lawu

Cetho Temple

A terraced Hindu temple of the late Majapahit period high on Mount Lawu, still used for prayer, with views over tea fields and misty valleys.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 about 1.5 hrs east of Solo
  • 🌅 Morning, clear weather
Our tipIt is a living place of worship: dress modestly, speak softly and follow local customs. It is cool up there, so bring a layer.
📷 Crisco 1492 · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#10UNESCOSragen

Sangiran Early Man Site & Museum

One of the world's key sites for early human fossils, inscribed by UNESCO in 1996. The museum shows bones and skulls found in the surrounding hills.

  • ⏱ 2 hours
  • 🚗 about 15 km, 45-60 min north of Solo
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipCheck the museum's opening days before going. The displays are interesting for older children and adults; a guide helps explain the finds.
📷 Ministry of Culture (Indonesia) · Public domain · Wikimedia Commons

Practical tips

Getting there

Adi Soemarmo Airport (SOC) is about 14 km north-west of the centre; an airport train runs to Solo Balapan station, or take a car. Trains from Yogyakarta, Jakarta and Surabaya also arrive in the city. From Yogyakarta International Airport there is no direct link - change in Yogyakarta, about 2.5-3.5 hours in all.

Best time

The dry season, roughly May to September, is best, especially for the mountain temples and waterfall. Big events include Grebeg Sudiro around Lunar New Year and Sekaten / Garebeg Maulud at the Kraton; their dates move each year, so ask us.

Respect & dress

Cover shoulders and knees at the palaces, temples and mosques, and use your right hand to give and receive. Solo is a courtly, gentle city, so keep voices low. Most local food is halal, but Chinese-Indonesian eateries around Pasar Gede may not be - ask first.

Getting around

The centre is compact: use ride-hailing apps, the Batik Solo Trans buses or a becak (agree the price first), and walk the batik alleys. Lawu temples, Tawangmangu and Sangiran are best by car with a driver.
