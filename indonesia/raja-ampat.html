The 10 places not to miss

Chosen for first-time and returning visitors. Add the ones you like to your trip - we turn them into a plan.

#01GatewaySorong

Sorong, the gateway

The port city where flights land and boats leave for the islands. Most visitors only pass through to stock up, but Doom Island nearby has old Dutch-era buildings.

  • ⏱ Transit, 1 night if needed
  • 🚗 airport about 8 km from the centre
  • 🌅 Arrival and departure days
Our tipFlight and boat times often do not line up, so plan a night in Sorong before and after the islands. We check the current ferry times for your dates.
📷 Lord Mountbatten · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#02Base townWaigeo Island

Waisai, the regency capital

The small capital of Raja Ampat on Waigeo island, with the marine park office, a market, shops, a hospital and a port where island boats depart.

  • ⏱ Transit
  • 🚗 about 2 hrs by ferry from Sorong
  • 🌅 Arrival day
Our tipEvery visitor needs the Raja Ampat marine park entry tag. It is required, and we explain how to get it. Keep it with you on every boat and bring cash.
📷 VerryFun · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#03Karst viewWaigeo, Dampier area

Piaynemo Viewpoint & Star Lagoon

A short climb up wooden steps ends at the classic Raja Ampat view: green limestone islets scattered across a blue lagoon. Star Lagoon is a quick boat hop away.

  • ⏱ Full day
  • 🚗 about 1.5-3 hrs by boat from Waisai
  • 🌅 Early morning
Our tipGo early when it is cooler and the light is soft. The steps are steep, so wear non-slip shoes and carry water. Sea conditions decide whether the boat can go.
📷 Rizalubun · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#04IconicNorthern Waigeo

Wayag Islands

The postcard image of Raja Ampat: a maze of mushroom-shaped limestone islets and turquoise water, seen from a steep viewpoint climb and best explored by boat.

  • ⏱ Full day or overnight
  • 🚗 about 4-8 hrs by boat from Waisai
  • 🌅 Calm months, early morning
Our tipVery remote, with a long open-sea crossing, a viewpoint trail without safety rails and little medical help. Use an experienced operator, travel in calm months and check current access rules.
📷 Elias Levy · CC BY 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#05Far southSouthern Raja Ampat

Misool

The southern island group of karst islets, hidden lagoons, caves and some of the best-protected reefs in Raja Ampat, reached by long boat transfers.

  • ⏱ 3+ days
  • 🚗 about 4-5 hrs by boat from Sorong
  • 🌅 October to April
Our tipMisool is the most weather-dependent area; strong winds in the middle of the year can stop boats. It is usually reached through a resort or liveaboard, so we check transfer days for your dates.
📷 Hulivili Pro-Mitglied · CC BY 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#06VillageDampier Strait

Arborek Village

A tiny island village with homestays, a long jetty and clear shallow water where soft corals and fish gather under the pier.

  • ⏱ 2-3 hours
  • 🚗 about 1-2 hrs by boat from Waisai
  • 🌅 Morning or late afternoon
Our tipTreat it as someone's home: dress modestly in the village, ask before taking photos and follow the community's rules about snorkelling and rubbish.
📷 https://www.flickr.com/photos/hulivili/ · CC BY 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#07DivingDampier Strait

Kri & Mansuar (Dampier Strait diving)

The heart of Raja Ampat diving: reefs around Kri and Mansuar teem with fish, with turtles, reef sharks, and manta rays at cleaning stations. Jetties also suit snorkelling.

  • ⏱ 1-3 days
  • 🚗 about 1-2 hrs by boat from Waisai
  • 🌅 October to April
Our tipCurrents in the strait can be strong. Dive only with a certified operator, tell them your experience honestly, and ask which sites suit snorkelers.
📷 Lord Mountbatten · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#08Reef wallDampier Strait

Friwen Wall & Islands

A reef wall that drops from a shallow plateau of hard corals, also a good snorkelling spot beside small islands and a village homestay.

  • ⏱ Half day
  • 🚗 by boat in the Dampier Strait, ask for time
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipCurrents can pick up on the wall, so divers should match the site to their level and use a certified guide. Snorkellers should stay near the boat and follow the crew.
📷 Fabian Lambeck · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#09Tide sandbankNear Kri & Arborek

Pasir Timbul Sandbank

A white sandbank that rises out of the sea at low tide, with shallow turquoise water around it for a swim, snorkel and photos.

  • ⏱ 1 hour
  • 🚗 a short boat hop from Arborek
  • 🌅 Low tide
Our tipIts size depends on the tide, so check the tide table. There is no shade, so bring a hat and sunscreen, and do not step on the coral around it.
📷 GGgord · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#10Karst bayWaigeo Island

Kabui Bay & Pencil Rock

A sheltered bay of karst islets and quiet channels near Waigeo, with Batu Pensil, a slim rock pillar standing alone in the water.

  • ⏱ Half day
  • 🚗 by boat from the Waisai area, ask for time
  • 🌅 Morning, calm water
Our tipA calmer, shorter alternative to far-away Wayag. Ask your boatman about tides and keep your hands inside the boat in narrow channels.
📷 prilfish · CC BY 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons

Practical tips

Getting there

Fly to Sorong (Domine Eduard Osok Airport, SOQ), about 8 km from the city. From Sorong harbour a fast ferry takes about 2 hours to Waisai, then a second boat reaches your island or resort. Raja Ampat is remote: boats and flights are needed, costs add up, and weather can change plans.

Best time

October to April usually has the calmest seas and best visibility, so it is the best window for Wayag and Misool. June to August is windier and rougher. A Raja Ampat marine park entry tag is required for every visitor - we explain how to get it.

Respect & dress

Island villages are small, mostly Christian communities and Sunday is a day of rest. Dress modestly on land, ask before photographing people, and never touch coral, feed fish or leave rubbish. Muslim travellers: Sorong has mosques and halal food, but island meals are mostly fish and rice, so tell us your needs early.

Getting around

Everything between islands is by boat, usually a chartered speedboat or one arranged by your homestay, resort or liveaboard. There is no daily join-tour system everywhere, so book ahead. Medical care outside Sorong and Waisai is limited, so carry insurance and your own medication.
