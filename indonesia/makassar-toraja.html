The 10 places not to miss

Chosen for first-time and returning visitors. Add the ones you like to your trip - we turn them into a plan.

#01Fort & museumMakassar city centre

Fort Rotterdam

A seafront fort first built by the Gowa kingdom and later rebuilt by the Dutch. Its old stone buildings and courtyards are now a calm place to learn about the history of Makassar.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre; about 30-45 min from the airport
  • 🌅 Morning or late afternoon
Our tipA museum is inside the fort and guides at the gate can explain the history. Go before the midday heat, and check opening times locally as they can change.
📷 Sanko. · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
🏞️#02Sunset & foodMakassar city centre

Losari Beach & Makassar food

Makassar's seafront promenade comes alive at sunset. Try pisang epe, grilled banana with palm-sugar sauce, and the city's famous beef dishes such as coto Makassar soup and konro ribs.

  • ⏱ 2-3 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre, a short ride from the fort
  • 🌅 Sunset and evening
Our tipThe promenade is busy at sunset, so keep bags close. Coto and konro are rich beef dishes usually eaten with rice cakes; ask about ingredients if you have dietary needs.
#03Pinisi harbourNorth Makassar

Paotere Harbour

The old harbour where traditional wooden pinisi schooners load cargo for islands across Indonesia. Fishermen, a fish market and colourful boats make it a lively place for photos.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 about 10 min north of Fort Rotterdam
  • 🌅 Early morning
Our tipEarly morning is busiest and most photogenic. Planks are uneven and wet, so wear shoes with grip, and ask crews before photographing them.
📷 Oceanmuse · CC BY 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#04Karst & riverMaros

Rammang-Rammang karst

A small boat glides along a quiet river between towering limestone peaks and rice fields, then a short walk leads to a village and a viewpoint over one of the world's largest tower karst areas.

  • ⏱ Half day
  • 🚗 about 40 km, 1.5-2 hrs from the city centre
  • 🌅 Morning or late afternoon
Our tipWear the life jacket on the small boat, bring a hat and water, and watch your step on wet paths. Go in the drier months for clearer skies.
📷 Chafidwahyu · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#05Waterfall & parkMaros

Bantimurung waterfall & butterflies

A waterfall, cool pools and forest in Bantimurung-Bulusaraung National Park, long known as the 'kingdom of butterflies' since naturalist Alfred Russel Wallace collected here in the 1850s.

  • ⏱ 2-3 hours
  • 🚗 about 50 km, 1.5 hrs from the city centre
  • 🌅 Weekday morning
Our tipIt gets crowded on weekends and holidays, so go on a weekday morning. Rocks near the water are slippery, and the river can rise quickly after rain.
📷 22Kartika · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#06Beach & boatsBulukumba

Bira Beach & Tana Beru pinisi yards

Tanjung Bira has soft white sand and clear water. Nearby Tana Beru is where wooden pinisi ships are built by hand on the beach, a craft listed by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.

  • ⏱ 1-2 days
  • 🚗 about 180-200 km, 4-6 hrs by road
  • 🌅 Dry season
Our tipIt is a long drive, so plan at least one night. Shipyards are working sites: ask permission and follow safety directions. Swim where locals do and ask about currents.
📷 Superhickish · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#07Tongkonan villageNorth Toraja

Ke'te Kesu' traditional village

A row of carved and painted tongkonan houses faces a line of rice barns on stilts. Behind the village, old coffins and wooden effigies rest in cliff graves, some very old.

  • ⏱ 1.5 hours
  • 🚗 about 4 km from Rantepao
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipThese are family burial places: do not touch bones or coffins, do not climb on graves, and ask before taking photos. A local guide adds a great deal of meaning.
📷 Ansensius · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#08Burial sitesToraja

Londa cave graves & Lemo cliff graves

At Londa, coffins and bones lie in a large cave under a cliff, visited with a lamp-carrying guide. At Lemo, rows of carved tau-tau effigies stand in balconies cut into a rock face.

  • ⏱ 2-3 hours
  • 🚗 about 20-30 min south of Rantepao
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipThe Londa cave is dark with uneven ground, so wear sturdy shoes and follow your guide. Treat the graves with respect and never remove or move anything.
📷 Michael Gunther · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#09HighlandsMount Sesean

Batutumonga highland views

A mountain village at about 1,300 metres on the slopes of Mount Sesean, with sweeping views over the Rantepao valley and rice terraces, often above a layer of early morning cloud.

  • ⏱ Half day
  • 🚗 about 1 hr uphill by road from Rantepao
  • 🌅 Early morning
Our tipThe road is steep and narrow, so go with a driver. Bring a jacket because it is cool up there, and start early, before clouds fill the valley.
📷 Kondephy · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#10MegalithsNorth Toraja

Kalimbuang Bori megalith site

A field of more than a hundred tall standing stones, each one raised for a funeral held here. Nearby are baby graves in a living tree and cliff graves.

  • ⏱ 1 hour
  • 🚗 about 20 min from Rantepao
  • 🌅 Morning, outside holiday peaks
Our tipRambu Solo' funerals are family events held mostly in the dry season. Join one only through a local guide and the host family's welcome, in dark modest clothes, and ask before photographing.
📷 JelajahSuwanto · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons

Practical tips

Getting there

Fly to Sultan Hasanuddin Airport (UPG) near Makassar, about 30-45 minutes from the city centre. Rantepao in Toraja is around 300 km, roughly 8 hours by road; a small plane also flies to Toraja Airport (Buntu Kunik) - we check the current schedule.

Best time

The dry season (roughly May to October) is best for roads, beaches and hikes. Toraja funeral ceremonies are mostly held in the dry months, with July and August busiest, so book early then.

Respect & dress

Makassar is mainly Muslim, with mosques and halal food everywhere; dress modestly. Toraja is mostly Christian and pork is common on menus, so Muslim travellers should ask for halal places in advance. At graves and funerals wear dark, modest clothes and follow your guide.

Getting around

In Makassar use ride-hailing apps, taxis or pete-pete minibuses. For Rammang-Rammang, Bantimurung, Bira and Toraja a car with driver is easiest; mountain roads in Toraja wind and can be slow. A local guide is strongly recommended in Toraja.
