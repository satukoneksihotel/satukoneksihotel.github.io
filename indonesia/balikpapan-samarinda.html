The 10 places not to miss

Chosen for first-time and returning visitors. Add the ones you like to your trip - we turn them into a plan.

#01City beachBalikpapan seafront

Melawai Beach, Balikpapan

Balikpapan's easy-going seafront promenade, where families stroll, kids play on the sand and food stalls open as the day cools. It is the simplest way to feel the city's relaxed, oil-port character after a day of travel.

  • ⏱ 1-2 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre
  • 🌅 Late afternoon into evening
Our tipThis is a city beach for strolling and snacking rather than a swimming spot, so judge the water by what you see. Evenings and weekends are busiest.
📷 Bloesafir Pamanjagau · CC BY 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
🏞️#02Stilt villageMargasari, Balikpapan

Kampung Atas Air (water village)

A neighbourhood of wooden houses on stilts over the bay, linked by plank walkways, where daily life happens on the water. Colourful walkways, boats tied up at the doorstep and a view across the bay make it a favourite for photos.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 about 15-25 min from the centre
  • 🌅 Morning or late afternoon
Our tipPeople really live here, so keep your voice down, ask before photographing residents and stay on the walkways. Planks can be slippery after rain, so wear flat shoes with grip.
#03Mangrove walkGraha Indah & Margomulyo

Balikpapan Mangrove Forest

Mangrove areas on the edge of Balikpapan Bay, where raised wooden boardwalks lead you among the tangled roots. They protect the shoreline and shelter birds, crabs and, with luck, proboscis monkeys - the long-nosed monkey that lives only on Borneo.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 about 20-30 min from the centre
  • 🌅 Early morning or late afternoon
Our tipWildlife sightings are never guaranteed. Go early or late when it is cooler, wear insect repellent and watch your step on wet boards. Which mangrove spots are open and how to enter varies, so we check.
📷 David Dennis · CC BY-SA 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#04Wildlife reserveSamboja, Kutai Kartanegara

Samboja Lestari orangutan & sun bear reserve

A conservation area run by the Borneo Orangutan Survival Foundation, where former rainforest is being replanted. Rescued orangutans live on forest islands and sun bears have their own sanctuary - a chance to learn about Borneo's wildlife and how it is protected.

  • ⏱ Half day
  • 🚗 about 1-1.5 hrs from Balikpapan
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipThese are rescued animals, not a zoo: visits are set by the organisation and may need to be arranged in advance, with limited days and group sizes. We check the current visiting rules for your dates before planning around it.
📷 Andrea.knox · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
🏞️#05BeachEast Balikpapan

Manggar Segarasari Beach

A long, open beach on Balikpapan's eastern side with a wide sandy shore, shady trees and picnic spots. It feels more spacious and local than the city seafront, and fits a relaxed half-day.

  • ⏱ 2-3 hours
  • 🚗 about 30-45 min from the centre
  • 🌅 Weekday mornings
Our tipWeekends and holidays get crowded with local families. Currents and waves can be strong, so check with staff or locals before swimming and keep children close.
#06Riverside mosqueMahakam riverbank, Samarinda

Samarinda Islamic Center

One of the largest mosques on Borneo, with a grand dome and a very tall minaret beside the Mahakam River. The river is the heart of Samarinda, and the mosque is the city's best-known landmark.

  • ⏱ 1 hour
  • 🚗 about 10-15 min from the centre
  • 🌅 Late afternoon, sunset
Our tipVisitors are welcome outside prayer times; dress modestly and remove shoes where asked. Whether the tower or inner halls are open to tourists changes, so we check before you go.
📷 Bloesafir Pamanjagau · CC BY 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#07WeavingSamarinda Seberang

Kampung Tenun & Samarinda sarongs

Across the Mahakam lies Samarinda Seberang, an older quarter known for its hand-woven sarongs, a craft long tied to the Bugis community here. Visit a weaving house, watch the wooden looms at work and bring home a sarong as a souvenir.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 about 15-25 min across the river
  • 🌅 Morning to early afternoon
Our tipWeaving is not always in progress when you arrive, so ask a local contact or us to arrange a time. Check the quality and the price before buying, and be polite about photographing the weavers.
📷 NeniRiska · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#08Sultanate historyTenggarong, Kutai Kartanegara

Tenggarong & Mulawarman Museum

The riverside town that was the seat of the Kutai Kartanegara sultanate. The Mulawarman Museum, in the sultan's former palace building from the Dutch period, keeps royal heirlooms and tells the story of the old kingdom.

  • ⏱ Half day
  • 🚗 about 1 hr from Samarinda
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipThe Erau festival, a traditional Kutai ceremony, is held in Tenggarong every year, but the dates vary - we check them for your trip. Museum opening days and any closures also change, so ask us first.
📷 Ezagren (bicara / talk) · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#09River dolphinsMahakam River, Kutai Kartanegara

Mahakam pesut (river dolphin) trip

A boat trip on the upper Mahakam to look for the pesut, the rare freshwater Irrawaddy dolphin, with a glimpse of riverside village life along the way. Seeing one is a special moment, but it is a matter of luck.

  • ⏱ Full day
  • 🚗 several hours by road and boat from Samarinda
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipSightings are not guaranteed and depend on water level and weather. Choose a boat operator that keeps its distance and a slow speed, and we check the trip details for your dates. It is a long day, so start early.
📷 Bernard DUPONT from FRANCE · CC BY-SA 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#10FoodBalikpapan & Samarinda

East Kalimantan food: crab, seafood & amplang

Both cities are good for fresh seafood, especially crab and prawns, cooked simply or in rich sauces. Look for amplang, the crunchy fish crackers the region is known for, and for dishes from the Banjar and Bugis communities that live here.

  • ⏱ 2 hours
  • 🚗 in both city centres
  • 🌅 Evening
Our tipAsk about the price before ordering seafood sold by weight. Most food is halal but some Chinese-run eateries serve pork, so ask first. Amplang makes an easy gift to bring home.
📷 Crisco 1492 · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons

Practical tips

Getting there

Balikpapan's Sultan Aji Muhammad Sulaiman Sepinggan Airport (BPN) is the main gateway to the region, usually with the most flights, and Samarinda has a smaller airport (AAP) with fewer routes. Balikpapan and Samarinda are about 2-3 hours apart by road depending on traffic, so most travellers fly into Balikpapan and drive to Samarinda, or the other way round.

Best time

Borneo is hot, humid and green all year, and rain can come at any time. The months from roughly June to September are usually somewhat drier, which helps with boat trips, mangrove walks and forest visits. Plan outdoor sights for the morning or late afternoon and carry a light rain jacket.

Respect & dress

Cover shoulders and knees at mosques and the palace museum, and ask before photographing people, especially in the water village and weaving houses. Keep your distance from wildlife, never feed it and follow rangers and boat crews. Most local food is halal and prayer rooms are easy to find, but some Chinese-run eateries serve pork, so ask first.

Getting around

In both cities use ride-hailing apps or taxis for short hops. Samboja Lestari, Tenggarong and the Mahakam villages are spread out, and boat trips need advance arrangements, so a car with driver is the easiest way to combine them. The Derawan islands and the new Nusantara capital area are far from here and need their own trips - ask us if you want to add them.
