The 10 places not to miss

Chosen for first-time and returning visitors. Add the ones you like to your trip - we turn them into a plan.

#01Island iconNusa Penida, west coast

Kelingking Beach (the T-Rex cliff)

A long, narrow headland that drops into the sea and looks like a dinosaur with its head raised, with a small white beach and bright blue water far below. It is the most photographed spot on Nusa Penida.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 roughly 1-1.5 hrs by road from the harbours
  • 🌅 Early morning or late afternoon
Our tipThe viewpoint is the safe and easy part. The path down to the beach is very steep, partly rough and slippery, and has hurt visitors - go down only if you are fit, wear proper shoes, and never in rain or when the sea is high. Midday is hot and crowded.
📷 Chainwit. · CC BY 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#02Sea carved rockNusa Penida, west coast

Angel's Billabong & Broken Beach

Two sights a short walk apart on the same stretch of cliff. Broken Beach is a round hole where the sea has worn through the rock into an open arch, and Angel's Billabong is a clear natural rock pool that sits right at the cliff edge.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 roughly 1-1.5 hrs by road from the harbours
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipPlease treat the billabong as a place to look at. Waves can surge over the rock without warning and have swept people away, so we do not suggest swimming there, and access may be closed when the sea is rough. Rock edges are slippery and have no fences.
📷 Haryanti · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#03Sunset beachNusa Penida, west coast

Crystal Bay

A pretty curved bay of pale sand and clear blue water, with a few small rocky islets offshore. It is one of the easier beaches to reach, good for a swim or a snorkel, and a favourite place to watch the sun go down.

  • ⏱ 2-3 hours
  • 🚗 roughly 1 hr by road from the harbours
  • 🌅 Late afternoon, sunset
Our tipCurrents and waves can be strong on this side of the island, so swim near the shore and check the sea first. Divers and snorkellers usually go with a boat operator; we can arrange one for your dates.
📷 Panina Manina · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#04Hidden coveNusa Penida, east coast

Atuh Beach

A secluded cove on the east side, reached by a long stairway down a green cliff. A small rock island stands just offshore, and the beach is usually quieter than the famous spots on the west coast.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 roughly 1.5 hrs by road from the harbours
  • 🌅 Morning, around low tide
Our tipThe stairs are long and steep, and you climb all the way back up in the heat - bring water. The surf here can be strong, so paddle with care. Tides change how much sand you can walk on, so we check them for your day.
📷 Hendrojkson · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#05White sand & stairsNusa Penida, east coast

Diamond Beach

A bright strip of white sand under tall limestone cliffs on the island's southeast tip. A stairway cut into the cliff leads down to turquoise water, and the view from the top is one of the best on Nusa Penida.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 roughly 1.5 hrs by road from the harbours
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipThe steps are steep and can be slippery, with a handrail in places - go slowly, especially coming back up. The sea here is for paddling rather than swimming because of surf and currents. Combine it with Atuh Beach, close by.
📷 Stryn · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#06Manta raysNusa Penida, south coast

Manta Point snorkel or dive

A boat trip to a cleaning station on the south coast where large manta rays often glide past close to the surface. Snorkel trips usually pair it with another reef stop, and qualified divers can go deeper.

  • ⏱ Half a day by boat
  • 🚗 boat trip from Penida or Lembongan
  • 🌅 Morning, calm seas
Our tipMantas are wild animals, so a sighting is likely but never promised. Swell, wind and strong currents can make it unsuitable or cancel the trip, and it needs confident swimmers. Keep a respectful distance and never touch the animals. We check conditions and operators for your dates.
📷 Rilando June Lamadjido · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#07Wave sprayNusa Lembongan, southwest

Devil's Tears (Lembongan)

On the southwest edge of Nusa Lembongan, big waves surge into hollows in the black cliff and burst up in sheets of spray. Dream Beach and a small sunset viewpoint sit close by, so it is an easy stop on a loop around the island.

  • ⏱ 1 hour
  • 🚗 roughly 15-20 min by scooter or car from the village
  • 🌅 Late afternoon, sunset
Our tipThe show is best when swell is big, which is also when it is dangerous. Stay behind the railings, keep children close, and step back from wet rock - freak waves can reach further than they look. Spray can soak your phone and bag.
📷 User:(WT-shared) Burmesedays · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#08Quiet paddleNusa Lembongan, northeast

Lembongan Mangrove Forest

A green belt of mangroves on the island's quiet northeast side. Small boats, kayaks and paddle boards glide through shaded channels, and the area is a good break from the busier beaches. Seaweed farms nearby show how many families here earn a living.

  • ⏱ 1-2 hours
  • 🚗 roughly 10 min from the main beach
  • 🌅 Morning, around high tide
Our tipTides decide whether boats can get in, so we time the visit for your day. Wear a hat and sunscreen, move quietly so the birds stay, and do not break roots or leave rubbish behind.
📷 User:(WT-shared) Burmesedays · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#09Island hopLembongan to Nusa Ceningan

Yellow Bridge & Blue Lagoon (Ceningan)

A narrow yellow suspension bridge links Nusa Lembongan with tiny Nusa Ceningan, and the lanes beyond it lead past seaweed farms, small cafes and cliffs. On the Ceningan side, the Blue Lagoon is a patch of deep turquoise water against the rocks.

  • ⏱ 2-3 hours
  • 🚗 roughly 10-15 min from Lembongan's main beach
  • 🌅 Morning or late afternoon
Our tipThe bridge is narrow and shared by scooters and walkers, so go slowly and give way. Ceningan's lagoon has strong currents and rocky edges - enjoy the view, and do not jump from the cliffs. Mind the sun and bring water.
📷 Juxlos · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#10Cave templeNusa Penida, near Sakti

Pura Goa Giri Putri (cave temple)

A Hindu temple inside a large limestone cave. After a climb up a stairway, you squeeze through a small opening in the rock and find a tall chamber with shrines and quiet places to pray.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 roughly 45-60 min by road from the harbours
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipIt is a working place of worship, so wear a sarong and sash (often lent at the gate), speak softly and follow local custom. The entrance is tight and the cave is dim, so skip it if you are claustrophobic. It may be closed during ceremonies.
📷 Sasuke shinozawa · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons

Practical tips

Getting there

Both islands are reached by boat from Bali, mostly from Sanur, and also from Padang Bai. Ngurah Rai airport (DPS) is the usual starting point on Bali. Boats depend on sea conditions, so crossings can be delayed or cancelled when swell and wind are up - we arrange transfers and check conditions for your dates, and suggest a spare day around flights.

Best time

The dry season, roughly April to October, usually gives clearer skies and calmer crossings, though it is also busier, especially in July and August. Seas can still be rough in any month, and the wetter months bring heavier rain and more cancellations. Manta rays can be seen through much of the year; the ocean sunfish is usually a mid-year visitor.

Respect & dress

Wear a sarong and sash at temples, step around the small offerings on the ground, and keep voices low during prayers. The coast is wild, so respect fences and warning signs at cliffs and rock pools - they are there for a reason. Please take your rubbish with you and use reef-friendly sunscreen near the coral.

Getting around

Nusa Penida's roads are rough, narrow and steep in places, and journeys take longer than the map suggests. A car with a local driver is the safest and most comfortable choice; scooters are only for experienced riders, with a helmet and care on wet or sandy bends. Lembongan and Ceningan are small enough to explore by scooter, bicycle or on foot.
