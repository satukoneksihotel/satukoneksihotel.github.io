The 10 places not to miss

Chosen for first-time and returning visitors. Add the ones you like to your trip - we turn them into a plan.

#01GatewaySimalungun

Parapat, gateway to the lake

A cool lakeside town on the eastern shore of Lake Toba. It has viewpoints over the water and Samosir island, and it is where most boats and ferries to the island depart.

  • ⏱ A few hours or overnight
  • 🚗 about 4-5 hrs by road from Medan
  • 🌅 Morning, before the ferry
Our tipPassenger boats to Tuktuk leave from Parapat, while car ferries cross from Ajibata to Tomok. Times change, so we check the current schedule for your day.
📷 hotmahtg · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
🏞️#02Lake staySamosir

Tuktuk & Samosir lakeside

A peninsula on Samosir with most of the island's guesthouses, cafes and lakeside gardens. Swim, kayak, rent a bicycle or scooter, and watch the sun set over the water.

  • ⏱ 1-3 days
  • 🚗 about 45 min by boat from Parapat
  • 🌅 Sunset, dry months
Our tipUse a helmet on rented scooters - roads are narrow and hilly. Wear a life jacket on boats, and avoid the water when afternoon winds make the lake rough.
#03Batak heritageSamosir

Tomok & the Sidabutar royal tombs

Near the ferry landing stand the stone sarcophagi of the Sidabutar kings, whose clan is counted among the first settlers of Samosir. Stalls nearby sell ulos cloth and carvings.

  • ⏱ 1 hour
  • 🚗 about 5 km from Tuktuk
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipThese are family tombs: dress modestly, speak quietly and ask your guide before taking photos. Agree any guide arrangement before you start.
📷 Christian Advs Sltg · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#04Batak heritageSamosir

Ambarita stone chairs (Huta Siallagan)

In a walled village, stone chairs and a table sit under a large old tree, where village elders met some 300 years ago to judge crimes. A guide explains the village and its customs.

  • ⏱ 1 hour
  • 🚗 about 5 km north of Tuktuk
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipGo with the local guide, who tells the history, including some dark stories that may not suit young children. Wear sun protection, as there is little shade.
📷 Ahanpranata · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
🏞️#05MuseumSamosir

Simanindo & Huta Bolon museum

At the northern tip of Samosir, a restored Batak royal house is now a museum of carved wood, textiles and old objects, set among traditional buildings by the lake.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 about 15 km north of Tuktuk
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipTraditional dance shows are not held every day, so ask us or your guesthouse before you go. Combine it with the lake road drive around the island.
#06WaterfallKaro Highlands

Sipiso-piso waterfall & Tongging

A thin waterfall of about 120 metres that plunges into a green gorge at the northern end of Lake Toba. Viewpoints at the top look over the lake, and a steep path leads down.

  • ⏱ 2-3 hours
  • 🚗 about 1-1.5 hrs by road from Berastagi
  • 🌅 Morning, clear weather
Our tipThe walk to the base takes about 30-45 minutes down and longer back up, and the steps can be slippery. Take the viewpoint only if you are short on time or energy.
📷 Airil anuar · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#07Volcano hikeKaro Highlands

Berastagi & Mount Sibayak

Berastagi is a cool highland town known for its fruit and vegetable market. Nearby Mount Sibayak is an easy-to-moderate volcano hike to a steaming crater, followed by a soak in hot springs.

  • ⏱ Full day
  • 🚗 Berastagi is about 66 km, 2-3 hrs from Medan
  • 🌅 Early morning start
Our tipHike with a local guide, start early before clouds come in, and keep away from sulphur fumes at the crater. Mount Sinabung nearby is active, so we check the official volcano status first.
📷 Palma Bernardo Alexius Hutabarat · CC BY 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons
🏞️#08OrangutansLangkat

Bukit Lawang orangutan trek

A riverside village at the edge of Gunung Leuser National Park, where guided walks through the rainforest look for semi-wild Sumatran orangutans, along with monkeys and tropical birds.

  • ⏱ 1-2 days
  • 🚗 about 3-4 hrs by road from Medan
  • 🌅 Morning trek
Our tipGo only with a licensed local guide, keep your distance (about 10 metres), and never feed or touch the animals. Skip the trek if you have a cold, as orangutans catch human illnesses.
#09Royal heritageMedan city centre

Maimun Palace & Great Mosque of Medan

Istana Maimun is the yellow palace of the Deli Sultanate, mixing Malay, Moorish and European styles. Close by, the Al Mashun Great Mosque has grand domes and decorated arches.

  • ⏱ 2 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre, close to each other
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipDress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered. The mosque is a working place of worship, so visit outside prayer times and ask at the entrance about rules for visitors.
📷 Airil anuar · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#10Heritage & foodMedan city centre

Tjong A Fie Mansion & Medan food

The grand home of Tjong A Fie, a Chinese-Indonesian merchant of old Medan, now a museum. Chinese, Malay and European touches show the mix of cultures that built the city.

  • ⏱ 1.5 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre
  • 🌅 Morning, then lunch
Our tipVisit in the morning, then eat: Medan is famous for its food, such as soto Medan, bika ambon cake and durian in season. Ask us for halal-friendly places near your hotel.
📷 Pratyeka · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons

Practical tips

Getting there

Fly to Kualanamu Airport (KNO), about 40 km from Medan city centre; an airport train runs to the city in about 45 minutes. Parapat is roughly 4-5 hours by road, Berastagi about 2-3 hours. Ferries cross from Parapat or Ajibata to Samosir.

Best time

The drier months are usually the most comfortable for lake days and hikes, but rain can come in any month, so plan outdoor visits for the morning. The lake and highlands are cool - bring a light jacket; Medan is hot and humid.

Respect & dress

Around the lake most Toba Batak are Christian and pork is common on menus; Muslim travellers should look for halal-marked places, which are easy to find in Medan and Parapat. Cover shoulders and knees at tombs and mosques, and ask before photographing people.

Getting around

On Samosir, hire a car with driver or a scooter (with a helmet) - roads are narrow and hilly. Between Medan, Berastagi and Bukit Lawang a car with driver is easiest. In Medan use ride-hailing apps, as traffic is heavy. Wear a life jacket on every boat.
