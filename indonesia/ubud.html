The 10 places not to miss

Chosen for first-time and returning visitors. Add the ones you like to your trip - we turn them into a plan.

#01Rice terracesTegalalang

Tegalalang Rice Terraces

Green rice paddies stacked down a river valley, shaped by the traditional Balinese water-sharing system. Paths wind between the steps, and cafes along the ridge look out over the palms.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 about 30 min north of Ubud
  • 🌅 Early morning
Our tipGo early, before the tour buses and the heat. Paths can be steep and slippery after rain, and some small donation points or swings ask for a fee, so check first.
📷 Thomas Fuhrmann · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#02Forest & templesPadangtegal

Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary

A shady forest in the middle of town, home to many long-tailed macaques and a few old Hindu temples with moss-covered statues and stone bridges. It is both a nature reserve and a place of worship.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 walkable from the centre
  • 🌅 Morning or late afternoon
Our tipDo not carry loose food, and keep sunglasses, hats and phone straps secure - the monkeys grab things. Never feed them or stare at them, and stay calm if one climbs near you.
📷 Mutante · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#03Palace & marketCentral Ubud

Ubud Palace & Ubud Market

The royal palace, Puri Saren, with carved gateways and courtyards, sits at the main crossroads, with Ubud's craft market just across the road. In the evening, traditional dance performances are often staged in the palace grounds.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 in the town centre
  • 🌅 Morning for the market, evening for dance
Our tipBargain politely at the market and compare a few stalls first. Dance programmes and tickets change from day to day, so we check what is on for your dates.
📷 Chainwit. · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#04Holy springTampaksiring

Tirta Empul Holy Spring Temple

A Hindu water temple built around a sacred spring, with clear pools and a row of spouts where Balinese come to purify themselves. Its roots are traditionally traced back to around the 10th century.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 about 40 min northeast of Ubud
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipIt is a working temple: wear a sarong and sash (often lent at the entrance) and follow the signs. If you want to join the purification, ask the staff how it is done, and it is fine just to watch.
📷 Chainwit. · CC BY 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#05Ancient caveBedulu

Goa Gajah (Elephant Cave)

An old hermitage cave whose entrance is carved with a fierce, wide-mouthed face and swirling foliage. Nearby are bathing pools with water spouts and a green, shady path down a small ravine.

  • ⏱ 1 hour
  • 🚗 about 15 min southeast of Ubud
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipThe cave inside is small and dark, and the steps down to the pools are uneven. It is a sacred site, so wear a sarong and sash, and combine it with a stop at nearby sights in Bedulu.
📷 Chainwit. · CC BY 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#06Easy walkCampuhan

Campuhan Ridge Walk

A gentle paved path along a grassy ridge between two river valleys, with open views of palms and hills. It starts near the old Campuhan bridge area and is a favourite for an easy morning stroll.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 walkable from the centre
  • 🌅 Sunrise or late afternoon
Our tipStart at sunrise or late afternoon to avoid the midday sun, and bring water and a hat. The path is easy for most people, but there is little shade along the ridge.
📷 Tigerente · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#07WaterfallKemenuh

Tegenungan Waterfall

A wide waterfall dropping into a green pool in a jungle gorge, reached by a stairway and a short path. It is one of the easier waterfalls to visit from Ubud, with viewing spots at the top and bottom.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 about 25 min south of Ubud
  • 🌅 Morning, dry season
Our tipThe stairs down are steep and you have to climb back up, so wear shoes with grip. Rocks are slippery and the current can be strong after heavy rain, so follow the signs and staff.
📷 Karter873 · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#08Lotus pond templeCentral Ubud

Pura Taman Saraswati (Water Palace)

A small temple dedicated to Saraswati, the goddess of learning and the arts, set behind a pond full of lotus flowers. Its carved gateway and floating shrines make it one of Ubud's prettiest photo spots.

  • ⏱ 45 min-1 hour
  • 🚗 in the town centre
  • 🌅 Late afternoon, evening
Our tipThe best light is in the late afternoon, and dance shows are often held here in the evening. Visitors usually look from the entrance and the walkways, so respect any areas marked for worshippers.
📷 Chainwit. · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#09Rock-cut shrinesTampaksiring

Gunung Kawi Temple

Tall shrine-like reliefs carved into the rock face of a river valley, thought to date from around the 11th century. A long stairway through rice fields leads down to the carvings, the water and a small temple.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 about 40 min northeast of Ubud
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipThe stairway has many steps and the climb back is tiring in the heat, so bring water. It is a sacred site, so wear a sarong and sash. Easy to pair with Tirta Empul on the same trip.
📷 Chainwit. · CC BY 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#10Art & paintingCentral Ubud & Sanggingan

Ubud art museums: Puri Lukisan & Neka

Ubud has been an artists' village for a long time, and its museums show Balinese painting from classic wayang-style scenes to modern work, including pieces by foreign artists who lived here. Puri Lukisan and the Neka Art Museum are two well-known stops.

  • ⏱ 1-2 hours per museum
  • 🚗 5-15 min from the centre
  • 🌅 Midday, when it is hot
Our tipA good indoor plan for the hottest part of the day or a rainy afternoon. Opening days and hours vary and some galleries close on certain days, so we check before you go.
📷 Arabsalam · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons

Practical tips

Getting there

Ubud is inland, so most visitors fly into Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS) near Denpasar and drive up, usually about 1-1.5 hours depending on traffic. There is no train in Bali, so a private car, hotel transfer or taxi is the usual way. Ride-hailing pickups can be limited in some local areas, so we can arrange a transfer for you.

Best time

The dry season, roughly April to October, is the easiest time for terraces, walks and waterfalls. The rainy season, roughly November to March, is greener but wetter, with slippery paths and stronger waterfalls. July-August and the year-end holidays are the busiest, so book hotels early. Ubud is cooler than the coast, but still warm and humid.

Respect & dress

At temples wear a sarong and sash (often lent or rented at the entrance), cover shoulders if asked, and never climb on shrines. Small offerings are placed on the ground and doorsteps every day - step around them. During ceremonies, stay behind the worshippers and ask before photographing. Nyepi, Bali's day of silence, usually falls in March; the whole island goes quiet, so we check the dates when planning.

Getting around

The centre of Ubud, with the palace, market, Saraswati temple and Monkey Forest, can be done on foot, but pavements are narrow and the sun is strong. The rice terraces, Tirta Empul, Gunung Kawi, Goa Gajah and the waterfall are spread out, so a car with driver is the easiest way to combine them. Scooters are common to rent, but traffic is busy, so we suggest a driver unless you are an experienced rider.
