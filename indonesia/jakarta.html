The 10 places not to miss

Chosen for first-time and returning visitors. Add the ones you like to your trip - we turn them into a plan.

#01LandmarkMerdeka Square, Central Jakarta

Monas, the National Monument

A tall marble tower topped with a gold-covered flame, standing for Indonesian independence. A lift goes up to an open viewing deck, and a history museum with dioramas sits below.

  • ⏱ 2-3 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre
  • 🌅 Morning (cooler)
Our tipQueues for the lift can be long, so go early. Opening days and hours change, and it may be closed one day a week - check before you go.
📷 Gunawan Kartapranata · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#02Old BataviaOld Town, West Jakarta

Kota Tua & Fatahillah Square

The old Dutch colonial heart of Batavia. Fatahillah Square is ringed by the Jakarta History Museum, in the former city hall, and by cafes, with museums close by.

  • ⏱ 2-3 hours
  • 🚗 about 20-40 min from the centre
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipThe square is flat and walkable - rent an old-style bicycle if you like. It gets hot and busy by midday and at weekends, so start in the morning.
📷 Chainwit. · CC BY 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#03Two faithsCentral Jakarta

Istiqlal Mosque & Jakarta Cathedral

Southeast Asia's largest mosque faces a neo-Gothic Catholic cathedral across the road, linked by an underground tunnel - a symbol of Indonesia's religious harmony.

  • ⏱ 1.5-2 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre, next to Monas
  • 🌅 Morning
Our tipVisit outside prayer times, especially Friday midday. Cover shoulders and knees; robes are usually lent. Shoes come off before the prayer halls, and a guide may be offered at the mosque.
📷 Midori · CC BY 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#04Historic portNorth Jakarta

Sunda Kelapa Old Harbour

One of Jakarta's oldest ports, where tall wooden pinisi schooners still load cargo. A short ride from Kota Tua, it is a vivid, working slice of the city's sea-trading past.

  • ⏱ 1-1.5 hours
  • 🚗 about 10 min from Kota Tua
  • 🌅 Morning light
Our tipIt is a working harbour with uneven planks and heavy lifting - watch your step and follow the workers' directions. Ask before photographing people.
📷 CEphoto, Uwe Aranas · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#05MuseumCentral Jakarta

National Museum of Indonesia

Indonesia's main museum of archaeology and ethnography, known as the Elephant Museum for the bronze elephant at its gate, with ancient statues, textiles and crafts from across the islands.

  • ⏱ 2-3 hours
  • 🚗 in the city centre, facing Merdeka Square
  • 🌅 Midday (indoors, air-conditioned)
Our tipThe museum reopened in October 2024 after a fire, and restoration of parts was still planned - some halls may be closed. Check what is open for your dates.
📷 Gunawan Kartapranata · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#06Culture parkEast Jakarta

Taman Mini Indonesia Indah

A large park built around traditional-style pavilions for Indonesia's provinces, with museums, a lake with a model of the archipelago, a cable car and places of worship.

  • ⏱ Half day
  • 🚗 about 45-90 min from the centre, depending on traffic
  • 🌅 Morning to afternoon
Our tipIt is big and hot, so wear comfortable shoes and use the park transport. It is a good first look at regional cultures if you will not travel beyond Java.
📷 Gunawan Kartapranata · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#07Family funNorth Jakarta

Ancol Dreamland & the bayfront

A big seaside leisure area on Jakarta Bay with the Dunia Fantasi theme park, an aquarium, a beach promenade, restaurants and the marina where island boats leave.

  • ⏱ Half day to full day
  • 🚗 about 30-60 min from the centre
  • 🌅 Late afternoon
Our tipWeekends and school holidays are very crowded - weekdays are calmer. The water of Jakarta Bay is not clean enough to count on for swimming, so enjoy the rides and the sea views.
📷 Michael.starlight · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Wikimedia Commons
#08Island escapeThousand Islands (Kepulauan Seribu)

Thousand Islands day trip

A chain of small islands off Jakarta's coast with white sand, snorkelling and quiet stays, reached by speedboat from Marina Ancol - a fast break from the city.

  • ⏱ Full day, or overnight
  • 🚗 boat from Marina Ancol about 1-1.5 hrs
  • 🌅 Weekdays, dry season
Our tipBoat times are limited and trips can be cancelled in rough weather. Weekends are crowded, and some islands may only accept resort guests - we arrange the right island and boat for you.
📷 Rochelimit · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons
🏞️#09Food & nightlifeSouth Jakarta

Blok M & Kemang food trail

South Jakarta's dining neighbourhoods: Blok M with its Japanese-style eateries and street food, and Kemang with a mix of cafes and restaurants for a relaxed evening.

  • ⏱ Evening
  • 🚗 about 30-60 min from the centre
  • 🌅 Evening
Our tipMost places are halal, but check the sign or ask - some bars and restaurants serve pork or alcohol. Use ride-hailing at night rather than walking far.

Practical tips

Getting there

Soekarno-Hatta Airport (CGK) is west of the city - allow roughly 45 minutes to 1.5 hours by road depending on traffic, or take the airport train. Long-distance trains arrive at Gambir, and the Whoosh high-speed train from Bandung stops at Halim in the east.

Best time

The dry season (roughly May to September) is the most comfortable and best for boat trips. The rainy season (about November to March) can bring heavy downpours and local flooding. It is hot and humid all year - plan outdoor sights for the morning.

Respect & dress

Jakarta is mostly Muslim: halal food is everywhere, malls and offices have prayer rooms, and many places slow down at Friday midday prayers. Dress modestly in mosques, take shoes off where asked, and use your right hand to give and receive. Ask before photographing people.

Getting around

Traffic is heavy, especially in the morning and evening rush hours. The MRT, commuter trains and TransJakarta buses avoid the jams, and ride-hailing apps are cheap and easy. For a day with several stops, a car with driver is the most relaxed choice.
