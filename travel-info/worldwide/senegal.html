Entry

Most sources say Indonesian citizens need a visa for Senegal, applied for at a Senegalese embassy before you travel (allow several weeks); one site claims visa-free entry, which the others do not support. The usual documents are a passport valid for 6 months, photos, a return ticket, proof of accommodation and a yellow fever certificate, and an entry authorisation may be required. Confirm the current rule with the Senegalese foreign ministry or an embassy before you book. Ask a doctor about malaria prevention.

Top cities and places to visit

Where most visitors go, and the places travellers find most attractive - based on tourism rankings and official tourism sites (checked October 2026).

1DakarMost visited

The vibrant capital and main gateway, with busy markets, live music, surf beaches and the African Renaissance Monument.

2Gorée IslandMost visited · Attractive

A short ferry from Dakar, this UNESCO island remembers the Atlantic slave trade through its House of Slaves.

3SalyMost visited · Attractive

The main beach resort on the Petite Côte, with family hotels, calm sea and wildlife reserves nearby.

4Saint-LouisAttractive

A UNESCO colonial island city of faded pastel houses, fishing boats and a well-known jazz festival.

5Cap SkirringAttractive

A quieter beach resort in green Casamance, with wide sandy beaches and traditional villages to explore.

6Sine-Saloum DeltaAttractive

A UNESCO delta of mangroves, islands and bird-filled lagoons, best explored by traditional pirogue boat.

Culture and practical - take note

Senegal is over 90% Muslim and famous for 'teranga' - warm hospitality. Greet people properly and ask after their family; use the right hand. Dress modestly, especially outside tourist beaches. Friday is the main prayer day. Halal food is everywhere. In markets and on beaches expect persistent sellers - a polite 'no merci' works. Ask before photographing people. Keep copies of your passport and avoid walking alone at night. Swim only where it is safe - Atlantic currents are strong. Gorée Island is a memorial of the slave trade: be respectful. Some governments advise caution in the Casamance region and near the borders - check advisories. French and Wolof are spoken.
