Entry

Visitors are generally given a free 30-day tourist stay on arrival, but you need a confirmed booking at a registered resort, hotel or guesthouse, an onward or return ticket, enough money for your stay and a passport valid for at least 6 months (guides vary - plan for 6). Everyone must also complete the official online traveller declaration (IMUGA) before arrival and departure. Use only the official immigration sites - there are many copycats.

Top cities and places to visit

Where most visitors go, and the places travellers find most attractive - based on tourism rankings and official tourism sites (checked October 2026).

1MaleMost visited

The compact capital and arrival point, with the Old Friday Mosque, a fish market and speedboats to nearby resorts.

2North and South Male Atolls (Kaafu)Most visited · Attractive

The atoll around the capital, with the most resort beds, short speedboat transfers and good surf and snorkelling spots.

3Ari Atoll (Alif Alif and Alif Dhaalu)Most visited · Attractive

A popular resort atoll west of Male, known for year-round whale shark sightings and manta ray dives.

4Maafushi (local island)Attractive

A local island in South Male Atoll with guesthouses, a tourist bikini beach and day trips, suited to budget travellers.

5Baa AtollAttractive

A UNESCO Biosphere Reserve known for Hanifaru Bay, where manta rays gather in large numbers in the wet season.

6Raa AtollAttractive

A growing resort atoll north of Male, reached by seaplane, with quiet islands, house reefs and overwater villas.

Culture and rules - take note

The Maldives is a fully Islamic country. Alcohol is only available on resort islands and licensed boats - not on local islands - and importing alcohol, pork, idols or anything contrary to Islam is forbidden. Bikinis are fine on resort islands and designated tourist beaches, but on local islands dress modestly in the village and swim in the marked 'bikini beach' area. Friday is the day of prayer; during Ramadan, avoid eating or drinking in public on local islands. Protect the reef: do not touch or stand on coral, and do not take shells, coral or sand. Boat and seaplane transfers follow fixed daylight schedules, so allow buffer time. A green tax is charged per person per night - ask your resort. Sun is strong: use reef-safe sunscreen.
